The press conference was, as usual, a lesson in saying almost nothing. The dot-plot said everything.

Jerome Powell held rates steady for the fifth straight meeting. His statement changed very little. His tone was cautious, data-dependent, and studiously neutral. If that were the whole story, markets would have yawned.

But the Summary of Economic Projections — the quarterly document where every Fed official anonymously marks where they think rates should be — pulled forward. The median participant now sees two cuts by year-end. In March, that number was one.

### What changed

Three things shifted on the committee:

The core inflation forecast ticked down two-tenths. Unemployment projections nudged up. And crucially, the distribution of individual dots tightened — fewer outliers holding firm above 5%, more clustering at 4.50% and below.

In Fed-speak, that's not a pivot. But it's the clearest pre-pivot signal of this cycle.

### How markets read it

Two-year yields dropped fifteen basis points in the hour after release. The dollar softened. Growth names outperformed. Mortgage-backed securities — the most rate-sensitive corner of fixed income — had their strongest session in weeks.

### What to watch

The next CPI print on May 14. If it comes in at or below 0.2% month-over-month, the path to a July cut gets very real. If it surprises hot, today's dot-plot was a head-fake, and the June meeting becomes the one everyone rewatches.
