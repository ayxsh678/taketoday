Nvidia's data-center business did $40.1 billion in a single quarter. That's not a number — it's a weather pattern.

Revenue grew 62% year-over-year. Gross margin held above 74%, which is what happens when demand so outstrips supply that price isn't a variable. Guidance for the current quarter came in $1.5B above the street's high end.

And yet the post-market move was muted. Up 3%, then flat. The quiet reaction is the real headline.

### Why the restraint

The bull case is obvious: AI training and inference are the biggest capex cycle of the decade, and Nvidia owns the picks-and-shovels layer. The bear case has quietly gotten more sophisticated.

Four customers — the hyperscalers — now make up roughly 45% of data-center revenue. Any one of them pausing a cluster build-out would show up as a negative print for Nvidia, regardless of end-demand fundamentals. Analysts spent the call asking, in increasingly direct terms, when that bend arrives.

Jensen Huang's answer was the one he's given for eighteen months: demand exceeds supply into 2027. Convincing in 2024. Slightly less novel each time he says it.

### What to watch

Q3 guidance from Microsoft, Meta, Google, and Amazon on their respective earnings calls. If even one signals a capex moderation, the Nvidia trade — and everything priced off it, from power utilities to data-center REITs — gets a genuine stress test.
