For seven years, Indian fintech has been built on a promise: UPI is free. That promise is, quietly, up for review.

The Reserve Bank of India and the NPCI are consulting on reinstating merchant discount rates — the fee a merchant pays on a transaction — for specific high-value or enterprise-merchant UPI payments. The current proposal, per people familiar with the drafting, targets transactions above ₹2,000 at tier-one commercial merchants first, leaving peer-to-peer and small-business flows untouched.

### Why this is different

Previous trial balloons on UPI pricing landed with public outcry and were withdrawn within days. This one is different in two respects. First, it's been quietly workshopped with PhonePe, Google Pay, and the bank consortiums for months. Second, the political framing has shifted: it's being positioned as a sustainability measure, not a new tax.

### Who it hits

The obvious losers are the payments apps themselves, whose economics improve if MDR exists. The less obvious losers are the adjacent startups — loyalty, BNPL, merchant SaaS — whose GMV assumptions bake in zero cost-to-serve on payments. A 0.3% MDR at enterprise scale is a nine-figure line item that nobody currently underwrites.

### What to watch

The NPCI's formal notification window. If it opens in the next sixty days, a Q4 implementation is live. If it slips past the monsoon session of Parliament, the proposal likely gets deferred again — and another year of zero-MDR assumption gets locked in.
