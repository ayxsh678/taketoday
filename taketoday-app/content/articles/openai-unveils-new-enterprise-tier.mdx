The product announcement that matters this year isn't a model. It's a contract.

OpenAI is rolling out an enterprise tier aimed at the customers it has historically struggled to close — banks, hospital networks, regulated infra — by leading with the things those buyers actually ask about first. Data residency in named regions. Audit logs that survive a subpoena. Version pinning so a silent model update doesn't break a clinical workflow overnight.

Notably absent from the pitch: new capabilities. Enterprises already have access to the frontier models. What they didn't have was a paper trail their compliance team would sign off on.

### What's actually in it

The tier is less "product" and more "procurement-grade SKU." Features include:

- Dedicated capacity with published SLAs, not best-effort rate limits.
- Private endpoints in specific geographies for data-residency mandates.
- Customer-controlled encryption keys.
- A named engineering contact, not a support inbox.

None of that is flashy. All of it is the stuff enterprise deals actually live or die on.

### Why it lands now

For the last year, the biggest friction in enterprise AI wasn't capability. It was legal. Procurement teams were sending back standard API terms with redlines the vendor couldn't accept. A tier built explicitly to answer those redlines — with the redlines already priced in — short-circuits twelve-month sales cycles.

The competitive read: this is OpenAI attacking Microsoft and Anthropic where both are already strong, by making the paperwork story louder than the model story. In procurement, the paperwork usually wins.

### What to watch

Whether the tier comes with true audit rights — the ability for a customer's security team to actually inspect what ran — or whether it stops at logs the customer can read but not verify. That distinction decides whether this is a real compliance offering or a dressed-up marketing one.
