The AI investment narrative of 2023 was about foundation models. Whoever built the biggest, most capable model would capture the enterprise. The enterprises would buy access, fine-tune, and deploy.

That narrative is colliding with enterprise procurement reality.

## What the procurement process actually selects for

A hospital system buying AI for radiology doesn't evaluate GPT-5 vs Claude 3. It evaluates whether the vendor is HIPAA-certified, whether their models have been validated on radiology datasets, whether they carry liability insurance for misdiagnosis, and whether the system integrates with the existing PACS and EHR stack.

A general-purpose model provider fails every one of those criteria out of the box. A vertical AI company built specifically for radiology — like Rad AI or Aidoc — passes all of them, often with existing customer references and regulatory clearances in place.

This pattern repeats in legal (Casetext, Harvey), financial services (Rogo, Daloopa), and construction (Alice Technologies, Versatile). In each case, the vertical AI company wins because they've done the domain work that a horizontal provider cannot afford to do at the depth required.

> "We evaluated six AI vendors. The general-purpose ones gave us impressive demos. The vertical one gave us a SOC 2 report, a HIPAA BAA, and three case studies from systems our CIO recognized. It wasn't close." — CIO at a regional health system, January 2026.

## The data flywheel that makes this durable

Vertical AI companies aren't just winning on compliance. They're building data flywheels that general models can't replicate. Every clinical note, legal brief, or financial model they process becomes training signal for the next version of their model.

The horizontal model providers face a fundamental tension: the data that would make their models domain-expert-level is exactly the data their enterprise customers won't give them to train on. Vertical AI companies, by contrast, often own their training data through exclusive partnerships, proprietary datasets, or first-mover agreements with industry incumbents.

That data exclusivity is the moat. The compliance wrapper is table stakes. The combination is a durable business.

The trillion-dollar question is whether OpenAI and Anthropic build vertical products themselves or partner with vertical players. The evidence so far suggests they'll try to do both — and probably do neither as well as the specialists.
