Google processes 8.5 billion searches per day. It will process 8.5 billion searches per day next year. The threat to Google isn't that people stop searching. It's that the searches that remain aren't worth as much.

This is the nuanced version of the "AI kills Google" narrative, and it's more accurate than either the panicked or the dismissive reading.

### The query migration pattern

High-intent research queries — "best project management software for a 50-person team," "compare term life vs whole life insurance," "what are the tax implications of an LLC vs S-corp" — are moving to AI assistants. These are the queries where someone has a specific decision to make and needs synthesized information, not a list of links to evaluate.

These queries are precisely the ones that advertisers pay $8–$40 per click to appear alongside. A software company bidding on "best project management software" is bidding for access to someone actively evaluating competitors. That's the most valuable commercial intent that exists in the search ecosystem.

When that query moves to ChatGPT or Perplexity, the commercial intent doesn't disappear. The purchase is still going to happen. But Google no longer captures the advertising moment, and the AI assistant captures a referral relationship instead.

> "We cut our Google Ads budget for research-intent keywords by 35% this year. The quality of leads from those keywords degraded because our target customers are doing that research in AI tools now. We followed the audience." — CMO at a B2B SaaS company, anonymized interview, February 2026.

### Google's own AI is part of the problem

AI Overviews — Google's attempt to answer queries directly in the search results — has reduced click-through rates on the organic links by an estimated 30%. The feature is Google cannibalizing its own SEO ecosystem to retain the research query before users leave for an AI assistant.

The economics are uncomfortable: AI Overviews require significantly more compute per query than traditional search, generate no ad revenue on zero-click queries, and reduce the click volume that drives publisher revenue, which reduces the content quality that makes Google's index valuable. It's a defensive move that degrades the product while protecting market share.

### The fragmentation outcome

The search landscape in five years isn't "Google is dead, AI won." It's permanent fragmentation across use cases:

Conversational AI dominates research, comparison, planning, and decision-support. Traditional search dominates local discovery, navigational queries, and real-time information where recency matters. Specialized platforms — Reddit for community knowledge, TikTok for product discovery among younger demographics, LinkedIn for professional context — own their respective query types.

Google wins the navigational and local slice definitively. It competes in real-time search with a genuine advantage (index freshness). It loses the research query segment, which is the highest-CPM advertising segment, permanently.

The dollar impact on Google's business is real and growing. It is not existential. Google is becoming a more ordinary business — high revenue, lower growth, facing competition it cannot simply outspend away. That's a significant change for a company that has known only dominance.

### What the challengers actually need

Perplexity and ChatGPT need to solve the advertising or monetization problem. A subscription-only model caps their addressable market significantly below Google's. The referral fee model — charging publishers for citations — is nascent and antagonizes the web ecosystem that provides their training data.

The AI search challengers are winning the product battle. They have not yet built a business model that scales to Google's revenue base. Google has a revenue model that is structurally degrading. Neither company is in a comfortable position.

The winner of the next decade of search is whoever solves the monetization of high-intent AI-mediated queries first.
