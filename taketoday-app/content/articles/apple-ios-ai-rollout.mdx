Apple is shipping its AI features the Apple way: late, narrow, and on its own hardware.

The headline pitch, confirmed by internal documentation reviewed by multiple outlets, is that the vast majority of user-facing AI in the upcoming iOS update — summarization in Mail, smart reply in Messages, priority notifications, Photos search — runs entirely on-device. A smaller, more capable "Apple Intelligence Cloud" handles queries that exceed on-device capability, but only with explicit per-request opt-in.

### The design choice that matters

What's conspicuously absent: a default ChatGPT or Gemini integration. Third-party models are supported, but they're a user-initiated detour, not a silent fallback. That's not an accident. It's the same pattern Apple used with the App Store, with Apple Pay, and with Apple Silicon — own the substrate, meter third-party access.

For users, the practical result is that AI features work even on airplanes, in subways, and in regions with patchy connectivity. For the industry, the strategic result is that Apple is building a moat on silicon the others don't have.

### Why it matters

If Apple's on-device model is genuinely competitive at the tasks normal people actually do on phones — and early benchmarks suggest it's close — the economics of the AI business start to look different. Cloud inference is expensive. Device inference is already paid for. Apple isn't selling AI as a subscription. It's using AI to sell phones.

### What to watch

Battery-life impact in real-world testing. If on-device inference tanks battery by 15%+, Apple has a marketing problem that dwarfs the privacy story.
